# Campaign funding and lobbying

**The rule:** money and votes are shown side by side as facts. Nothing on the site says or implies that money caused a vote. Avoid words like "bought", "paid for", "in the pocket of" anywhere, in code, copy or docs. Every number says what it counts, the period it covers and its source.

## What's shown

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| Rep profile, **Funding** tab (members of Congress) | Top PAC contributors by name; outside spending for and against; top contributing industries (approximate); totals raised, spent, cash on hand; the small-donor / larger-donor / PAC / party / self-funding breakdown; the average for the same chamber; donors' employers (3 or more donors). The current and previous two-year period (`?cycle=2024#funding`). |
| Bill page, **Follow the money** (bills in Congress) | Organizations whose lobbying reports mention the bill, the number of reports and the reported amounts (which cover the whole report); and, for the visitor's own reps (district cookie), each rep's final-passage position beside contributions in that two-year period from the industries that lobbied, with the note "This shows a relationship in the data, not a cause." |
| Rep page, **Funding** tab, **From lobbyists and lobbying organizations** (members of Congress) | LD-203 contributions honoring the member in the two-year period: organizations by name (with their PACs), each with its total, number of contributions and dates; registered lobbyists' own contributions only as a count of contributions and of lobbyists, and a total. |
| Bill page, **Follow the money**: organizations that lobbied on the bill and also gave | For each organization whose LD-2 reports mention the bill and whose own LD-203 reports list contributions to members of Congress in the same Congress (matched by lobbying registration ID): its lobbying (reports, clients, latest period and filing date) and, beside it, its contributions to each member with their dates. Two facts, never cause and effect. |
| California bill page, **Follow the money** | Lobbyist employers (Form 635) and organizations spending $5,000 or more (Form 645) whose quarterly reports list the bill: reports, total lobbying payments (the whole report, not one bill), latest quarter and filing date; then, side by side, the contributions those organizations reported to legislators' committees in the same session. |
| California legislator, **More → Lobbying** | Contributions to the legislator's campaign committees since 2025 reported by lobbyist employers, lobbying firms (Form 625) and Form 645 organizations, by organization, beside each one's own lobbying payments and the bills it listed; lobbyists (Form 615) and people reporting under their own name only as counts and totals. |
| Methodology, `#funding` | Sources, categories, limits, the no-causation rule, and the legal restriction on FEC donor data. |

State legislators and county officials say plainly that their funding isn't shown yet. The executive branch is below.

## Sources and sync

Two steps in the sync Worker (`workers/sync/src/funding/`), last in the daily run:

- **`federal-funding`**: the [FEC API](https://api.open.fec.gov/developers/) with its own api.data.gov key (secret `FEC_API_KEY`), so funding and the votes steps don't share one key's hourly limit. Without it, funding borrows `CONGRESS_API_KEY` at half the pace (`FEC_SHARED_MIN_INTERVAL_MS`, 8 s). Members are matched to FEC candidate IDs through the public [congress-legislators](https://github.com/unitedstates/congress-legislators) crosswalk (`unitedstates.github.io`, weekly), then each member's principal campaign committee for the period. Per member and period:
  - `/candidate/{id}/totals/` (all authorized committees),
  - `/schedules/schedule_a/` line `F3-11C` (PAC and other committee contributions, every page, summed by committee),
  - `/schedules/schedule_a/by_employer/` (up to 300 employers; never donor names),
  - `/schedules/schedule_e/by_candidate/` (independent expenditures for and against).

  **Order:** first the members who represent a live community (`LIVE_HOUSE_DISTRICTS`, `CA-5`, plus that state's two senators; keep it in step with `LIVE` in `functions/_lib/geo.js`), then the rest of those states' delegations (California), then everyone else. Each member is matched to FEC IDs and then read (both periods) before the next, so a member's Funding tab fills in as soon as they're reached; until then it says "Funding data is loading". Refreshes of members already loaded come after every new member.

  **Sharing a round with lobbying:** while lobbying has bills waiting, funding stops when `LOBBYING_ROUND_SHARE` (0.4) of the round's remaining time is left, so `federal-lobbying` gets that time instead of a single request. Funding doesn't start a member-period with less than 90 s of its own time left.

  About 6 to 20 requests per member and period, one every 4 s (`FEC_MIN_INTERVAL_MS`; 900 an hour, under the key's 1,000), at most `FEC_DAILY_LIMIT` (3,000) a day. The first full load (about 535 members × 2 periods) takes about 3 days. Afterwards the current period is re-read every `FUNDING_REFRESH_DAYS` (7), the previous one every 90 days: roughly 600 requests a day. FEC's bulk files weren't needed; the API is fast enough. While the first load runs, the daily run continues in rounds, so the analysis step starts later in the day than usual.
- **`committee-bills`**: Congress.gov's bill list for each full committee of the current Congress (`/committee/{chamber}/{code}/bills`; the committee list is read monthly): a bill counts when the committee **acted** on it ("Reported By", "Reported Original Measure", "Markup By", "Hearings By …", "Discharged From"), never for referral alone. Saved in `bill_committee_actions` with the date; a bill not loaded yet is added with its Congress.gov title, up to `COMMITTEE_BILLS_DAILY` (400) a day. Each committee's list is read in full once, then only what changed since the last read.
- **`lobbying-contributions`**: lda.gov LD-203 reports (`contributions/?contribution_honoree=<last name>&filing_year=…`), for each member of Congress and each year of the current Congress so far, paced like `federal-lobbying` on its own cap, `LDA_CONTRIB_DAILY` (400) requests a day; each member-year is read again after `LOBBY_CONTRIB_REFRESH_DAYS` (45). Every item of every report found is matched against **all** members (`matchHonoree()` in `lobbying.js`: the honoree's last words are the member's last name and the first word the member's given name or a nickname of it; a bare last name only when one member has it; committees, parties and "X for Congress" are not people). An item is kept only on exactly one match. A lobbyist's own report keeps no name (`lobbyist_id` only, to count distinct lobbyists). Amended reports replace the original at read time (newest posted for the same registrant, lobbyist, year and period).
- **`federal-lobbying`**: [lda.gov](https://lda.gov/api/redoc/v1/) (the Senate's LDA database, which moved from lda.senate.gov; no key needed, `LDA_API_KEY` optional). For each bill of the current Congress with a final-passage vote **or committee action** (`bill_committee_actions`), newest activity first (the later of its last final-passage vote and its last committee action): `filings/?filing_specific_lobbying_issues="H.R. 4"` and `"H.R.4"`, for each year of the Congress, 25 reports a page, one request every 3 s (`LDA_MIN_INTERVAL_MS`), at most `LDA_DAILY_LIMIT` (1,200) a day. The search resumes where it stopped (`bill_lobbying_progress.cursor`), and each bill is searched again every `LOBBYING_REFRESH_DAYS` (30). Large bills (budget reconciliation, appropriations) have thousands of reports and take a day or more.

**`LDA_API_KEY`** is optional: everything above works without it. lda.gov publishes no request limit for anonymous use; if `sync_log` shows lda.gov refusing requests (HTTP 429), register for a free key at <https://lda.gov/api/register/> and set it as a Worker secret (`npx wrangler secret put LDA_API_KEY` in `workers/sync`); it's sent as `Authorization: Token <key>`.

### Dark money (`dark-money` step, `tools/load_nonprofits.py`; migration 0030)

**Paused on every page (October 2026)** while each label is checked against its public record: `darkMoney.on = false` in `functions/_lib/dark-money.js` hides the donor labels, the share line, Form 990 facts and Form 9 sections, and skips reading them. The sync step still collects. Set it back to `true` to show them again.

Plain-language explainer: the methodology page's **How dark money works** (`#dark-money`).

- **Super PAC donors.** For each outside spender that reports its donors (any FEC committee type but I), for each period it spent in: its total receipts (`/committee/{id}/totals/`) and its **organization** receipts from Schedule A (`is_individual=false`, lines `F3X-11AI/11B/11C/12/17`, largest first, up to `DARK_MONEY_MAX_PAGES` (10) pages of 100). `aggregateDonors()` in `src/funding/darkmoney.js` counts only contributions (line 17 only when the filer labels it a contribution, so interest and refunds aren't donors), drops memo entries, and never lists a person (an individual, a name filed as "LAST, FIRST", or a family/revocable trust). Each donor is labeled: **Political committee** (a `contributor_id`; it discloses its own donors; links to its FEC page), **LLC** (by its name), **501(c)(4)** / **501(c)(6)** (its IRS record through ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer search, `pickNonprofit()`: exact name and state, one match only, else no label; links to the IRS record), or an organization (links to the FEC report page). The page's line: "[X]% of this group's reported money came from organizations that don't disclose their own donors" (LLC + 501(c)(4) + 501(c)(6) contributions ÷ total receipts), "At least" when not every receipt was read or not every donor looked up (only the largest 15 are). Tables `outside_donors`, `outside_donor_reads`, `org_lookups`.
- **Nonprofits.** Spenders of type I and Form 9 filers are looked up the same way (`fec_committees.state`, `.ein`). Each 501(c)(4)/(c)(6) spender or donor gets its latest Form 990 figures from ProPublica's API (revenue, total expenses, tax year; `nonprofits`). Political spending comes from the IRS's own e-file XML: `tools/load_nonprofits.py` (weekly "Refresh nonprofit filings" workflow) finds each return in the IRS index for this year and last, reads just that file from its batch zip with HTTP range requests (`tools/irs990.py`), and stores Schedule C Part I-A political expenditures and the political organizations Part I-C says it paid. No Schedule C → "no political spending reported (no Schedule C filed)"; not found → a link, never guessed. Pages say plainly that a 990 doesn't list donors.
- **Electioneering communications (Form 9).** Every row of `/electioneering/` for the current and previous period (a few dozen each), read daily, and each Form 9's own donation total (`/filings/?file_number=`). Shown under outside spending on a member's Funding tab (by `fec_candidates.candidate_id`) and on a candidate's Funding tab (by FEC ID): spender, amount (the share attributed to the candidate), dates aired, and "No donors reported" or "Donors listed on Form 9". No side is assigned to an ad.
- **Caps:** `DARK_MONEY_FEC_DAILY` (400) FEC requests a day through the funding steps' paced client; ProPublica paced at `PROPUBLICA_MIN_INTERVAL_MS` (1 s), `PROPUBLICA_DAILY_LIMIT` (300). Largest spenders first; a period's donors are read again after 30 days.
- **Tests:** `test/darkmoney.test.mjs` (in `npm test`) and `python3 tools/test_irs990.py`.

### California lobbying (`tools/load_ca_lobbying.py`, weekly)

The "Refresh California campaign data" workflow downloads the Cal-Access export for `build_ca_campaign.py`, then `tools/load_ca_lobbying.py` loads lobbying from the same file straight into D1 (only filings new or amended since the last run, `ca_lobby_loaded`):

- `CVR_LOBBY_DISCLOSURE_CD`: the cover of each Form 635 (lobbyist employer), 645 (organization spending $5,000 or more), 625 (lobbying firm) and 615 (lobbyist) since 2025, the latest amendment of each; a quarter with a mistyped date (a year like 2101) is left out. Reports filed under a person's own name are not loaded as reports.
- `SMRY_CD`: Form 635 line `ABCD` (total payments in connection with lobbying) and Form 645 line `AB` → `ca_lobby_reports.amount`.
- `LBY_ACTVTY` (the filer's own words) → the bills, by `bills_in()` in `tools/ca_lobbying.py`: AB, SB, ACA, SCA, AJR, SJR, ACR, SCR; a prefix carries over ("AB 6, 7, 96"); "ABs 238, 683"; fiscal years ("2026-27") aren't bills; a mention about implementing an earlier law ("SB 54 implementation", "regulations implementing SB 253 & SB 261") is set aside. Each bill gets the id of the session its quarter falls in (`ca-20252026-ab-6`) → `ca_bill_lobbying`.
- `LCCM_CD` (Forms 635 Part IV B, 625 Part IV B, 645 Part III B, 615 Part 2): contributions to candidates' committees → `ca_lobby_contributions`; matched to legislators through `state_campaign_committees` (their committees' Cal-Access filer IDs). Lobbyists and anyone filing under their own name are stored without a name.

`python3 tools/load_ca_lobbying.py <dbwebexport.zip> --mode measure` reads and counts without writing (October 2026's export: 25,503 employer reports since 2025, 12,323 listing bills, 73,630 bill mentions, 2,880 contributions).

Each mention is checked in `lobbying.js`: the whole number ("H.R. 4" isn't "H.R. 40"), not next to another Congress ("118th Congress S 1071"), and not followed by a different bill's title ("H.R. 1, Lower Energy Costs Act" for this Congress's H.R. 1). The report's own words around the mention are kept as the excerpt.

## The executive branch (`executive-funding`, migration 0009)

The same rules as for Congress, for every office whatever the officeholder's party. One step in the sync Worker (`src/funding/executive.js`), before `federal-funding`; pure parsers in `src/funding/disclosure.js` (tested in `test/disclosure.test.mjs`).

| Office | Funding tab |
|---|---|
| President | The FEC campaign money (the same tab as a member of Congress, from the President's `P…` candidate ID), the inaugural committee, and OGE financial disclosures. |
| Vice President | When their term began with the President's (they ran on one ticket): the same campaign figures as the President's, said plainly, since the FEC records the ticket's money under the presidential committee; the inaugural committee; OGE financial disclosures. |
| Cabinet | No campaign money (appointed), said plainly; OGE financial disclosure reports and ethics agreements. |
| California's statewide officers and all 120 legislators | Campaign finance from Cal-Access (see below), and their Form 700 statements (FPPC), under More → Disclosures. |

- **Outside spenders and "Donors not disclosed":** each committee in `funding_outside` (Congress and the President) is looked up once at the FEC (`/committees/?committee_id=…`, 50 at a time, again after 180 days) into `fec_committees`. A spender of FEC committee type **I** ("Independent expenditure filer (not a committee)") files Form 5 and doesn't have to report its donors except those who gave for those ads: its spending is labeled **Donors not disclosed**, and each list says how much of its total came from such groups. A person spending their own money (filed as "LAST, FIRST") is shown as "An individual, spending their own money (name not shown)", never by name, and isn't labeled. Super PACs and other committees report their donors and aren't labeled. See the methodology's `#donors-not-disclosed`.
- **Inaugural committee:** the committee named "inaugural" that first filed with the FEC in the 150 days before the President's term began (`/committees/?q=inaugural`). Its Form 13 reports (`/filings/?form_type=F13`), the latest version of each, with each report's own total (not added together: a later report can repeat earlier amounts). The main report's electronic file (`.fec` on docquery.fec.gov, one request, `FEC_FILES_DAILY_LIMIT` 20) is read line by line: donations (F132) summed, organizations by name, individuals only as a count and total (never named, never stored by name); refunds (F133) summed. Shown only when it adds up to the report's total within 5%. Checked every `INAUGURAL_REFRESH_DAYS` (7); the file is read again only when a new version is filed.
- **OGE (Office of Government Ethics):** its disclosure search API (`extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/API.xsp/v3/rest`, the JSON behind OGE's "Officials' Individual Disclosures Search Collection"), searched by last name, kept only when the first name matches too (`ogeNameMatches`; an official known by initials, like "J.D.", matches by the initials of OGE's given names, every initial). Each record: type as OGE lists it (Nominee or Annual 278e, 278 Transaction, Ethics Agreement, Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance…), the position and agency, the date OGE added it, and the document's link, or OGE's request form for documents released only on request (Form 201). Every `OGE_REFRESH_DAYS` (7), or the next day for an official with no match, one request every `OGE_MIN_INTERVAL_MS` (3 s), at most `OGE_DAILY_LIMIT` (200) a day.
- **California campaign finance (Cal-Access), migration 0012:** the **Refresh California campaign data** workflow (`.github/workflows/ca-campaign.yml`, weekly on Mondays and on demand) runs `tools/build_ca_campaign.py` on the Secretary of State's export (`campaignfinance.cdn.sos.ca.gov/dbwebexport.zip`, about 1.6 GB, rebuilt nightly; about 3 minutes), then `tools/ca_campaign_finish.mjs`, and commits `data/ca-campaign.json`. The `cal-access` step loads it when the file changes.
  - **Whose:** Form 460 cover pages (`CVR_CAMPAIGN_DISCLOSURE_CD`) from candidate and candidate-controlled committees (`CAO`, `CTL`). Statewide officers are matched by office and name (from `data/state-executive-officials.json`), since 2023. Legislators are listed by seat (`ASM-8`, `SEN-4`): every candidate with a statement for that seat since 2025; the step matches a sitting legislator to their seat's entry by last name and first name (`matchSeat` in `workers/sync/src/funding/state.js`; no unique match, no data, and the tab says so). An amendment replaces the statement it amends.
  - **Totals** by two-year period (2023-2024, 2025-2026): the sum of each statement's Summary Page (`SMRY_CD`, column A) line 5 (contributions received) and line 11 (expenditures made). Each statement's own totals stay listed with its PDF.
  - **Contributions** (Schedule A, `RCPT_CD`, form `A`; memo entries, transfers between the official's own committees and Schedule C left out): individuals only as totals and by employer (an employer listed only when 3 or more people named it; "Retired", "Not employed" and the like counted, never listed), never by name, at any amount. Committees, parties, businesses and other organizations by the name reported. Refunds (negative entries) net against the totals and never show as negative. Industries use the same keyword rules as federal funding (`industry.js`), applied in the Node step so the rules live in one place.
  - **Independent expenditures** for and against (Form 496 covers and `S496_CD` amounts), by spender, with the spender's committee page. Form 496 is filed only for $1,000 or more in the 90 days before an election, and the page says so. Reports that name the candidate without a district are matched only when one candidate of that name ran for that house.
  - Tested in `tools/test_build_ca_campaign.py` and `workers/sync/test/state-money.test.mjs`. **Cal-Access is replaced by CARS after the November 2026 election**; the workflow then needs the new system's data access.
- **California Form 700 (FPPC):** the FPPC's Form 700 search (`form700search.fppc.ca.gov/Home/SearchDocuments`, a JSON POST: `FilerLastName`, exact match). A statement is kept when the first name matches and one of its positions or agencies is the official's office ("State Controller" matches "Controller"; a legislator's is their house, "State Assembly" or "State Senate"); statements filed only for boards the official sits on are left out. Statewide officers first, then the live communities' legislators (`LIVE_STATE_DISTRICTS`, default `ca-assembly:8,ca-senate:4` for Calaveras), then the rest, `FPPC_PER_RUN` (30) a run. The FPPC's PDF links expire, so `/api/form700/<index ID>` (a Pages Function) asks the FPPC for a fresh link when a reader opens one and redirects to it, or to the FPPC's search if that fails. Every `FPPC_REFRESH_DAYS` (7), `FPPC_MIN_INTERVAL_MS` (3 s), `FPPC_DAILY_LIMIT` (100).

Tables: `fec_committees`, `inaugural_committees`, `inaugural_reports`, `inaugural_breakdown`, `inaugural_organizations`, `disclosures` (OGE and FPPC), `disclosure_checks`, `state_campaign_committees`, `state_campaign_totals`.

## Money from government, travel and foreign agents (migration 0031)

Shown beside funding and lobbying as facts on their own: never "in return", "after", "because", and nothing that ties an award, project, trip or contact to a vote or a contribution.

Loaded weekly by `tools/load_gov_money.py` (pure readers in `tools/govmoney.py`, tested in `tools/test_govmoney.py`) in the "Refresh government money" workflow (`gov-money.yml`; `--mode measure` reads and writes nothing; `earmarks`, `travel`, `fara`):

- **Earmarks** (`earmarks`, `earmark_requesters`, `earmark_requests`, `earmark_tables`). The Senate Appropriations Committee's table for each enacted law (`appropriations.senate.gov/download/fy<yy>-<sub>-cds-public-law`, both chambers' projects), read from `pdftotext -layout` text: a record starts on a line ending with its origination (H, S, H/S); requester cells split into House and Senate by column; a table that doesn't read in full stays a link (`status = 'link'`, e.g. FY2026 FSGG, scanned). The House committee's table of projects in its own bills (FY2027 on, `stage = 'house_committee'`) and its consolidated tables of members' requests (FY2024 on). Requesters match an official only on exactly one match (accents folded; "Torres (CA)"; a district settles a shared last name; a member who has since changed chambers is matched by first name and state). A request is tied to a table row (`enacted_id`) only when the same member is among its House requesters and the recipient is the same (or the row's project starts with it): one match only. Shown on the member's More tab (Earmarks, by fiscal year) and on an appropriations bill's page (`appropriationsOf()` reads the fiscal year and subcommittees from the title; a consolidated act's list is the tables', said plainly), with every project at `/laws/bills/<id>/earmarks/` (by state, 50 a page).
- **Federal awards** (`/awards/?name=`, `functions/awards/index.js`). Cloudflare's servers can't reach USAspending.gov (the TLS handshake fails, error 525), so the lookups run in GitHub Actions: `tools/load_gov_money.py awards` (the daily "Refresh federal awards" workflow, `awards.yml`, `--max-names` 3,500 a run) gathers every organization the site shows with a "Federal awards" link (`AWARD_NAMES`: donors' employers as the Funding tab lists them, each member's top 10 per period, bill lobbying clients, LD-203 organizations, super PAC organization donors, California lobbying organizations and employers, earmark recipients) and looks each up with `tools/usaspending.py` (tested in `tools/test_usaspending.py`): USAspending's recipient search, kept only on an exact name match (`name_key()`, the same rule as `nameKey()` on the page); one record per organization (parent, child and bare records with the same ID are one organization, shown by the record with the most money; up to three organizations, those with money first); obligations by awarding agency for the last four fiscal years. Stored in `federal_awards_cache`, looked up again after 30 days. The page reads only that; an organization opened before it was looked up goes into `federal_awards_wanted` (migration 0033) and is looked up first on the next run. Linked as "Federal awards" (`awardsLink()`).
- **Privately paid travel** (`gift_travel`). The House Clerk's gift travel search, by quarter since 2023 (sponsor, destination, dates, report; the member traveled when the filer is the member, otherwise "A staff member"), and the Senate's Gift Rule database (`giftruledata.zip`: member or staff, dates, report; the reports are scanned, so the sponsor and destination say "on the report"). Staff aren't named.
- **FARA** (`fara_registrants`, `fara_principals`, `fara_contacts`, `fara_docs`). The Justice Department's bulk files (registrants and foreign principals active since 2023; documents), and each supplemental statement filed since 2025 with a text layer (`--max-docs` new ones a run, newest first): its "Response to Item 12-Detail" table, keeping only contacts that name a member with their title or "Office of Senator/Representative X", one match only. Scanned statements stay links. On the member's More tab ("Contacts reported by foreign agents") and at `/foreign-agents/` and `/foreign-agents/<registration number>/`.

## Net worth, as reported (migration 0036)

"Net worth, as reported" for members of Congress, the President, Vice President, Cabinet and the justices: the range each annual financial disclosure report's assets and liabilities add up to. The same method and the same words for everyone; facts only (no rankings, "richest" lists, salary comparisons or "grew by X%"). Methodology: `/about/methodology/#net-worth`.

- **Loading** (`tools/load_networth.py`, readers in `tools/networth.py`, tested in `tools/test_networth.py` against real report text in `tools/fixtures/networth/`; the daily "Load net worth reports" workflow, `networth.yml`; `--mode measure` reads and writes nothing, `--names` limits it to some last names). Sources: the House Clerk's yearly indexes (`<year>FD.zip`, back to 2008: annual `O`, amended `A`, candidate `C`, new member `H`; attached to a member on one match, `load_trades.match_house`); the Senate eFD annual and candidate reports (back to 2012; paper filings are links); the OGE 278e reports the sync's `executive-funding` step lists in `disclosures` (annual, nominee, candidate; on request ones stay links until OGE posts them); the justices' reports in `data/scotus/trades.json`. OGE and the justices first, then newest first, `--max-reports` a run. A report read (or found unreadable) by the current `PARSER_VERSION` isn't read again; bump it when a change could read a report differently.
- **Reading.** Every asset and liability line from the text layer (`pdftotext -layout`; the Senate's e-filed HTML): House Schedules A and D (both layouts since 2008), 278e Parts 2, 5, 6 and 8, the justices' Part VII and VI (the 2025 list layout, and the table layout's value codes J–P4 before it). A report is `read` only when every line is accounted for (the House: one value per asset type code); an account or fund with holdings under it is counted once (a line with a value covers the lines under it; one without holds them); otherwise `unreadable`, with the reason, and it stays a link. House DocIDs not starting with 1, and OGE reports with no text layer, are `scan`; Senate paper filings `paper`.
- **Scanned reports** (`tools/networth_scan.py`, the "Read a scanned net worth report" workflow, `networth-scan.yml`, by hand only; migration 0037). For one report at a time (the President's newest annual 278e by default, or `report_id`): the report's own OCR text layer picks the pages with Parts 2, 5, 6 and 8 and the schedules attached to them (for the President's 2025 report, 178 of 928 pages; the 687 pages of transactions aren't sent); the PDF is split into chunks of 6 pages (`pdfseparate`/`pdfunite`); two models (`SCAN_MODEL_A`, `SCAN_MODEL_B`: Sonnet and Opus) each read every line as one compact string (`page|L|line|value|flags|creditor`, flags I income, E reported elsewhere, N not readily ascertainable, U hard to read); each chunk's reading is kept in `networth_scan_chunks`, so a run that stops resumes. Code compares the readings page by page (`networth.compare_readings`: same lines, same values and flags, nothing marked U), applies the text reader's rules (`networth.report_from_lines`) and checks every value against the page's OCR text (`networth.ocr_misses`; more than 2% missing holds the report). All agreed: written into `networth_reports` with `method = 'ai'`, but **not shown until a person confirms it** on `/admin/review/networth/` (`functions/_lib/networth-review.js`; `networthPoints()` lists an unconfirmed AI reading as a link, "read by AI, waiting for a person's check"); once confirmed it's labeled "Transcribed by AI from a scanned report, checked by [name]" under the chart. Any line read differently: nothing published (`networth_reports.status = 'scan'`, reason "Read by AI: …", shown as "read by AI, waiting for a person's check"); a person picks the matching reading for each line on that page, and `--mode publish` publishes with "checked by". Spending is capped (`--max-dollars`, 15 by default; `--mode estimate` counts the input tokens, free). The text loader never overwrites a report read this way. House scans and paper filings aren't read (the cost of thousands of reports).
- **The range.** Low = asset minimums − liability maximums; high = asset maximums − liability minimums. Open-ended values ("Over $50,000,000", a spouse's "Over $1,000,000", "Undetermined") count at their stated minimum and set `high_open` ("or more"; an open liability sets `low_open`, "or less"). Mortgages on a personal, primary or principal residence are left out (`residence_mortgages` counts them), since the home itself isn't reported.
- **Pages** (`functions/_lib/networth.js`, `rangeBands()` in `charts.js`). The summary line under the office line on `/reps/<slug>/` and `/justices/<slug>/` (also in Quick mode): "Net worth, as reported: $[low]–$[high] ([first year], [label]) → $[low]–$[high] ([latest year])", linking to More → Net worth, as reported. First: the earliest candidate or nominee report filed before taking office that was read ("before taking office"), else the earliest annual report read ("earliest report online", or "earliest report read" when an earlier report wasn't read). Latest: the most recent annual report read. Per year, the last filed report counts (an amendment replaces the original). No line when nothing has been read. The More section: "Estimated net worth: $X–$Y ([year]), as reported in ranges.", one band per year linked to its report, "Show the numbers", the limits note (always), and the reports not read, linked.
- **The law.** The Ethics in Government Act forbids commercial use of these reports: free, never sold, exported, offered through an API or put behind a paid plan.

## Industries

`src/funding/industry.js`: fixed keyword rules on a PAC's name or a donor's employer, the same for everyone; first match wins, narrower rules first. Unmatched names are "Not classified", and pages show what share of the money matched. Another member's leadership PAC or campaign committee comes from the FEC record (designation D, or committee type H/S/P), not keywords. Lobbying organizations use the same rules (with their self-description). Change the rules there; they're tested in `test/funding.test.mjs`.

## Privacy and the law

- No individual donor is stored or shown by name, at any amount. Individual giving appears only as totals, by size, and by employer, and an employer only when 3 or more donors named it.
- 52 U.S.C. 30111(a)(4): contributor information from FEC reports may not be sold or used to solicit contributions or for commercial purposes. There is no export, download or API of donor information, and none should be added.
