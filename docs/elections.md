# Elections

What's on the ballot, from official sources only. Pages: `functions/elections/[[path]].js` (plus the Elections section on Home, and "On the ballot" on county and district pages); shared code: `functions/_lib/elections.js`; data: `data/elections/<id>.json`.

## Where the data comes from

`tools/build_elections.py` builds `data/elections/<id>.json` (standard library plus `pdftotext` from poppler-utils). It runs in GitHub Actions, "Refresh election data" (`.github/workflows/elections.yml`), daily at 13:41 UTC and on demand, and commits the file when it changes. The official sites aren't reachable from everywhere, so don't expect it to run on a laptop or in a sandbox.

| What | Source |
|---|---|
| Contests, candidates, ballot designations, party preference, incumbent mark | Secretary of State, Certified List of Candidates (PDF) |
| Supreme Court and Court of Appeal retention questions, with each district's counties | The same list, at its end |
| Ballot order | The Secretary of State's randomized alphabet (its press release for the election); the method is on sos.ca.gov/elections/randomized-alphabet |
| Statewide candidate statements | Official Voter Information Guide, one page per office |
| Propositions: official title and summary (Attorney General), what a yes and a no vote mean, the summary of the Legislative Analyst's fiscal estimate, and each argument and rebuttal with its signers | Official Voter Information Guide: each proposition's page, its Official Title and Summary page (`title-summary.htm`) and its Arguments and Rebuttals page |
| Calaveras local contests | County's Qualified Candidates List (PDF), contests marked "On Ballot: Yes" |
| Calaveras candidate statements, Measure A (question, impartial analysis, tax rate statement, arguments) | County's Voter Information Pamphlet (PDF) |
| Santa Clara local measures (RTM and A–S: ballot question, jurisdiction, vote required, what was filed) | The Registrar of Voters' List of Local Measures, copied by hand into `data/elections/local/06085.json` (the county's site refuses automated requests from GitHub and Cloudflare); each question word for word; the impartial analyses and arguments are linked, not yet read, and the page says so. Its local candidate contests (73 on the ballot: school, college, city, water and sanitary districts) come from the Final Qualified List of Local Eligible Candidates, read from a copy by `tools/build_elections.py --local-candidates 06085 <pdf>` (names and ballot designations only; addresses, phones, emails and websites are never read). The list is in filing order, not ballot order, so these contests are listed alphabetically by last name and say the ballot order may differ (`list_order: "filing"`). Candidate statements are in the county's Voter Information Guide, not read yet, and the pages say so |
| U.S. House candidates' FEC filings | api.open.fec.gov (`FEC_API_KEY` secret, or DEMO_KEY), matched by last name and district |
| Results | api.sos.ca.gov/returns/ (read by the pages, never stored), only after `polls_close_utc` |

Rules the builder keeps:
- Every contest and measure keeps its `source_url`.
- No candidate contact details: the county's candidate list has addresses and phone numbers, which are never read into the file; lines in a statement giving an email or phone are left out, the same for every candidate, and the statement's note says so.
- Text from a PDF is extracted as printed (paragraphs rejoined) and the PDF stays linked as the official version.
- The pamphlet is laid out with two statements per page; headings are one or two centered lines, and names come with or without "AGE:". A statement is attached only to a contest whose list has that candidate. The log lists anything not matched.

To add a county: an entry in `COUNTIES` and `HOW_TO_VOTE` in the builder (its candidate list, pamphlet, results page, elections page and Board of Equalization district), and check the log after a run. To add an election: a new `ELECTION` block, then add its id to `ELECTIONS` in `functions/_lib/elections.js` (newest first).

## "Just the facts" (every measure)

Every ballot measure page (California's statewide and county measures, Washington's, and the "sections" states) leads with one card, `factsCard()` in `functions/_lib/measure-facts.js`, in the home page's card style (inner `.inset` panels, the type tokens, no new sizes), the same for every measure:

- **Chips**: the measure's type (statute, constitutional amendment, both, or a constitutional convention question), who placed it on the ballot (the Legislature or citizen initiative), and whether an official cost estimate is printed. The type and "placed by" chips are the official label's own words; the label itself (California's "LEGISLATIVE STATUTE.", "Put on the Ballot by …"; Washington's "Initiative to the People" and its ballot question; the other states' guide labels) is in each chip's title.
- **Yes** and **No**: two panels, word for word where the state prints them; side by side from 768px, stacked on phones.
- **What it changes**: California's Attorney General's summary, Washington's ballot title (Attorney General), Colorado's Blue Book summary, Florida's ballot summary, Michigan's ballot wording caption; otherwise the question on the ballot.
- **Cost**: the Legislative Analyst's estimate (California), the Office of Financial Management's summary (Washington), the Blue Book's fiscal impact (Colorado, cut to its first two paragraphs with their headings), a county tax rate statement's first paragraph.
- **One sources line** at the bottom, naming and linking each source once. Every fact in the card comes from one of them.

**Conflicts with other measures** (`measureConflicts()` in `functions/_lib/measure-facts.js`, the panel right after What it changes): only when a measure's official text says it can void, or be voided by, another measure on the same ballot. The daily election build saves that text in each measure's `conflicts` field (`[{text, title?, measures: ["prop-41"], source, source_url}]`, `tools/measure_conflicts.py`, tested in `tools/test_measure_conflicts.py`): for California, the Legislative Analyst's analysis first (in 2026 its passage on other measures is Figure 1, "Potential Interactions With Other Propositions", an image whose text the guide's web page carries as the image's text description; that text is quoted exactly as given, the guide's own spelling kept), and only when the analysis names no other measure, the official summary's conflict line ("States that other voter-approved measures appearing on the same ballot are void if they conflict with this measure and this measure receives more affirmative votes."); for the "sections" states (`tools/build_state_measures.py`), any official paragraph that speaks of the same ballot and names another measure on it by its label (none of the 2026 guides has one). The panel quotes the text, links each measure it names ("Names Prop 41, Prop 42."), and also shows, attributed, another measure's text that names this one when this one's own text doesn't name it back; the source joins the card's sources line. In Quick mode the card shows one line instead, "Can cancel or be cancelled by: Prop 41, Prop 42", linked (the quote itself when the text names no measure). A measure without such text shows nothing. On November 3, 2026: Prop 40's analysis names 41 and 42, and 41's and 42's each name 40. Tests: `workers/sync/test/measure-conflicts.test.mjs`.

Official text only: nothing written by ThePillory or AI, shortened only by cutting whole paragraphs, never by rewording; what the source doesn't print says so plainly. In Quick mode the card is all the page shows. The ballot preview (`/ballot/<st>/`, with and without an address) and Your ballot show the same card, folded under each measure's name (`factsFold()`). The campaigns' arguments and rebuttals follow, word for word, in one collapsed section, "What each side says (their own words, not checked by ThePillory)".

## Proposition titles

Any measure title printed entirely in capitals, in any state (today California's 14), is shown with ordinary capitalization, the words unchanged (`headlineCase()` in `functions/_lib/elections.js`: each word capitalized, small words such as "of" and "for" lowercase except at the start of a sentence, abbreviations and bill numbers like "U.S." and "ACA 13" kept), and one line says so wherever such a title appears (`titleNote()`: the measure page, the propositions list, Your ballot and the ballot preview). Any other title is shown as printed. The same rule as county candidate names, which the county prints in capitals.

## Who's behind each side, and who signed the arguments

**Who's behind each side** (`behindSection()` in `functions/_lib/measure-people.js`, a collapsed section before the arguments, the same layout for Yes and No): California's statewide propositions, from `data/elections/measure-money-2026-11-03.json`, built weekly by `tools/build_measure_money.py` in the "Refresh California campaign data" workflow from the Cal-Access export:

- **Committees formed for the measure**: ballot measure committees (entity BMC) whose campaign statements (Form 460) since late June 2026 (when the propositions were numbered) name the proposition by number, statewide, with a side, on the cover or the statement's list of measures. A committee naming both sides of one measure is left out. The export's own measure tables (`BALLOT_MEASURES_CD`, `FILER_LINKS_CD`) carry nothing for 2026, and the Cal-Access proposition pages are behind bot protection, so the statements are the source; each proposition's Cal-Access page is linked for readers.
- For each: raised and spent (Summary Page lines 5 and 11, column A, summed over its statements since 2025, latest amendment of each), cash on hand (line 16, latest statement), its ten largest donors that are organizations (by name, each linked to the latest statement it appears on), and individuals only as a total and a count, never by name, at any amount. "As of the statement covering through [date]". A committee formed for several measures says so: its totals cover all of them.
- **Independent spending by others**: Form 496 reports for or against the measure by anyone else (parties, general-purpose committees, organizations), by spender, with dates; people spending their own money are counted, never named.
- Other states: "Campaign committee data for [State]'s measures isn't loaded yet."

**Argument signers** (`signerDetails()`; `workers/sync/src/elections/signers.js`): names and titles exactly as printed. A signer is linked to their page only when their printed title names an elected office they hold now ("Senate Minority Leader", not "Former …") and exactly one official in the state's records, else exactly one certified candidate, has that name; a private person who shares a name is never linked. Each linked signer and each organization a title names ("President, [Organization]"; government bodies left out) gets a panel, closed by default, that opens in place:

- officials: office, party as plain text, final-passage votes on record and the latest, money raised this period, the net worth line, each with "Full record ›"; candidates: the race, party as listed, money raised (no votes or net worth on record);
- organizations: type from its IRS record through ProPublica (exact name and the election's state, one match only; looked up once by the sync's `measure-signer-orgs` step), California lobbying payments and years (Forms 635/645 under the exact name, one employer only), and its gifts to committees and its own spending on 2026 statewide measures, then "More ›".

Private individuals stay as printed, with no panel. No party labels for private individuals or organizations, no voter registration data, and nothing that ties money to a position in words.

## Washington's statewide measures

`tools/build_wa_measures.py` builds `data/elections/2026-11-03-wa.json` in the same shape as California's file, with only the three statewide measures (no candidates or local contests; `measuresOnly()` in `functions/_lib/elections.js` shows those elections as a list of measures, and "Your ballot" points to the statewide list, since it's the same on every ballot). It runs in the same daily workflow, after California. Sources, all from the Secretary of State's site:

| What | Source |
|---|---|
| Ballot title (statement of subject, concise description, the question) and the explanatory statement ("The Law as It Presently Exists", "The Effect of the Proposed Measure if Approved") | The Attorney General's letter to the Secretary for each measure (PDF) |
| Fiscal impact statement, its summary | The Voters' Pamphlet (one English edition; its statewide section is the same in every county's). The separate fiscal PDFs drop letters where their font uses ligatures ("e ect"), so they're linked, never read |
| Arguments for and against, each side's rebuttal, who wrote them | Each measure's Voters' Pamphlet page (two columns: supporters left, opponents right). Each side's "Contact:" line is left out |
| How to vote | VoteWA (register, track your ballot, where to vote) and the Voters' Guide |

Rules the builder keeps:
- **Word for word or nothing:** every paragraph it writes is checked against its source document (line breaks evened out); a paragraph that isn't there stops the build, and the file stays as it was.
- Text with a damaged ligature (`Ư`, `Ɵ`, "e ect") stops the build.
- "Rebuttal of argument against" is the supporters' rebuttal and "Rebuttal of argument for" the opponents', so each side's argument and rebuttal sit together, as on California's pages. The pamphlet's "Written by" applies to both.
- Paragraphs are rejoined from the PDF's lines: a paragraph ends on a short line or at a page break after a full sentence; a "Label:" line starts one; a campaign's heading (nearly every word capitalized) is its own paragraph. Tests: `tools/test_build_wa_measures.py`.

The measure URLs are listed in `MEASURES` in the builder. For the next election, add its documents there (they're linked from the Secretary of State's "Proposed ballot measure information" page and the Voters' Guide).

## The Constitution section (November 3, 2026 statewide measures)

Every statewide measure in `data/elections/2026-11-03-*.json` (California, Washington, Colorado, Arizona, Florida, Michigan: 44 measures) gets a collapsed "Constitution" section on its page, the same layout for every measure, between the official text and the arguments or results (`functions/_lib/measure-constitution.js`, migration 0041):

1. **What it changes:** constitution, statutes, both, a convention question, or "isn't stated", decided by the official label when it states one (`label_changes()`), with the official words quoted and their source linked.
2. **Constitutional provisions it touches:** U.S. clauses from `data/constitution.json` and California clauses from `data/ca-constitution.json` (built weekly from the California Legislative Information bulk export, `tools/build_ca_constitution.py`, "California Constitution" workflow), quoted word for word; other states' provisions linked to their official text. Claude suggests them; `check_draft()` in `tools/measure_constitution.py` keeps a provision only when its citation appears in the official text (U.S. clauses only when the text refers to the U.S. Constitution), every quote is found word for word, and each line passes the neutral line rule (`lineProblem()`). At most 8.
3. **Legal challenges:** CourtListener search (dockets and opinions) for the measure's label and state, filed since January 1, 2026: case name, court, docket, date filed, link. Never summarized.
4. The line: "This shows which parts of the Constitution each measure involves. It is not a recommendation on how to vote."

No aligns / tension panels before the election.

**Drafting:** the "Draft measure Constitution sections" workflow (`measure-constitution.yml`): `mode=draft` drafts every measure not yet drafted (`--redo` redrafts pending and sent-back ones with the reviewer's note; `--max-dollars` caps the spend), stores each as `pending` in `measure_constitution` with a history row, sets `sync_state.measure_constitution_total`, and queues the post-election analysis with each measure's official text. The daily run (`mode=cases`) refreshes court records for the 20 measures checked longest ago (CourtListener allows the token 125 searches a day, shared with the sync's order-courts step; each measure takes two; a run stops at the limit and the draft run stops searching there too): an unapproved measure's list is replaced; an approved measure's new records go to `cases_new` and wait for a person, so nothing comes down.

**Review:** `/admin/review/measures/` (Access): every measure with its notes, quotes and sources; keep or drop each provision and case, edit a line, approve (after ticking "I've read every quote") or send back with a note. **Nothing is public until every counted measure is approved**; then they all go live together (`publishedSection()`).

**After the election:** from November 4 (`not_before`), the sync's analysis step drafts the full three-panel analysis of every queued measure, passed or not (`workers/sync/src/analysis/measures.js`, `MEASURE_ANALYSIS_DAILY`, default 10), with the same checks and review as bills (`bill_analyses`, keyed `measure:<election>/<measure>`); a public one shows on the measure page under the Constitution section.

## The ballot preview (every state)

**The FEC list is never the ballot once the primary has passed.** FEC filings list everyone who filed, including people who lost a primary or withdrew. For each office (U.S. Senate, U.S. House), `nominations()` in `functions/_lib/election-window.js` reads the state's primary, runoff and convention dates from `data/elections/dates.json`: only while one is still ahead does the FEC list appear under "Federal races" (labeled "Before the primary"). Once they've all passed (or none is listed), the race shows no names: "[State] chose its nominees for this office in the primary on [date]. Enter your address to see the candidates on your ballot.", the address button, the state's official candidate list (its election office, from USA.gov), and "Filed with the FEC in 2026" as a separate link to the race page, "Not the ballot". California is unaffected: its races come from the certified list.


Every link and button to it reads "Preview [State]'s ballot", with the state's full name ("Preview California's ballot"; "Preview D.C.'s ballot"). On `/ballot/<st>/` the heading is "[State]'s ballot" and the statewide preview comes first: a small "Add your address for your exact ballot ↓" link, then (where ThePillory has the certified list, California) every statewide office in ballot order and every proposition, then the federal races and ThePillory's own ballot pages. Below them, the optional "Your exact ballot" card ("Add your address for local contests and where to vote.", a "Show my ballot" button), then "Always confirm your ballot with your county election office." and the official links. The "Preview [State]'s ballot" label is only ever on the button or link that leads to the page, never on the page itself. Once an address is entered, the heading is "Your ballot preview" (`previewLabel()` and `RESULT_HEADING` in `functions/_lib/election-window.js`). The URLs stay `/ballot/` and `/ballot/<st>/`.

`/ballot/` sends a visitor to their state's page (`functions/_lib/visitor-state.js`), or lists every state; `/ballot/<st>/` is `functions/ballot/[[path]].js`.

**Where the entry point sits follows the election calendar** (`functions/_lib/election-window.js`). From `WINDOW_DAYS` (45) before the next election in the visitor's state through Election Day, it's a small card on the home page and every state page just below the map: one line with the date and countdown ("Election Day: Tue, Nov 3 · 25 days"; "tomorrow", "today") and a standard-size "Preview [State]'s ballot" button, nothing else. The rest of the time it's a regular link in "Your state", the Elections section and the state page. Each state's own dates come from `data/elections/dates.json`, built daily by `tools/build_federal_races.py` from the FEC's election dates: primaries, runoffs, special elections and the general election, so a state with a different primary or a December runoff gets its own window. A special election counts only for the visitor's own U.S. House district (when their districts are saved). Runoffs say they're held only for races no one won outright. "Today" is the state's own date (its main time zone).

**Before an address** (one page for the state, private because it reads the districts cookie):
- **California** uses the Secretary of State's Certified List of Candidates instead of the FEC list: only the candidates on the November ballot, in ballot order, with party preference and ballot designation as certified (a top-two primary leaves two per race, where the FEC list still has everyone who filed).
- **Federal races** elsewhere from `data/elections/federal-2026/<st>.json`, built daily by `tools/build_federal_races.py` in "Refresh election data" (`FEC_API_KEY` secret; FEC's DEMO_KEY is too rate-limited for 52 places). The U.S. Senate race only when the FEC's list of 2026 races (`/v1/elections/search/`) has one for the state (a regular or special election); candidates are the FEC's statutory candidates (`candidate_status=C`: registered and past $5,000) active in 2026, alphabetical, name and party as filed. The House race shows once the visitor's district is saved. The page says plainly that this isn't the certified ballot.
- **Incumbents** link to `/reps/<slug>/#votes` and `#funding` through `fec_candidates` (the FEC candidate ID), never by name.
- **Official links:** the state's election office from USA.gov's directory (`data/states/election-offices.json`, built weekly by `tools/build_election_offices.py` in "Refresh state officials"; it writes nothing if it finds fewer than 50) and Vote.gov's home page (its per-state paths couldn't be checked). California, Washington, Colorado, Arizona, Florida and Michigan add their How to vote links from their election files.
- **ThePillory's own pages:** California's Your ballot and whole ballot; Washington's, Colorado's, Arizona's, Florida's and Michigan's statewide measures.
- Puerto Rico has no general election in 2026 (it votes in presidential years), and its page says so.

**With an address** (`POST /ballot/<st>/`, field `address`): `voterInfo()` in `functions/_lib/civic.js` calls Google's Civic Information API `voterinfo` with `returnAllAvailableData=true` and the `GOOGLE_CIVIC_API_KEY` secret (set on the Pages project). `ballotFromVoterInfo()` keeps contests in `ballotPlacement` order, candidates in the order listed (name and party only: no phone, email, site or social accounts), referendums (title, subtitle, brief, official link), polling places, early-voting sites, drop-off locations and the state's and county's official links (http(s) only). The answer is labeled "Your ballot preview for [the address as entered]"; the address is shown on that one answer only.

**With a ZIP code alone** (the same form, `ZIP` in `functions/ballot/[[path]].js`): a ZIP isn't enough for an exact ballot (it can span several districts, and the Civic API works from a street address), so it's never sent to Google and never counted against the daily limit. `data/zip/` gives the county (or counties) and every district part the ZIP covers ("95120 is in Santa Clara County and covers parts of these districts…", `zipCoverage()`), then the banner "A ZIP can span several districts; add your street address for your exact ballot.", the address card, the statewide races and measures, and the federal races (the House race only when the whole ZIP is in one district). Headed "What's on ballots in [ZIP]", never "your ballot".

**What the visitor enters always wins.** No lookup's answer reads the saved districts cookie: a failed lookup says so for what was entered ("There's no ballot information for "[entered]" yet") and never shows the saved place instead, and ThePillory's "Your ballot" links (which read the cookie) aren't offered on it. Every answer starts with "Showing: [what was entered]", plus "Use my saved place ([County]) instead" (a link to `/ballot/<st>/`) only when a saved place exists and differs (`zipIsSaved()`: every part of the ZIP, or the address's ZIP as Google read it, in the saved districts). A lookup never changes the cookie; "Make this my place" posts what was entered to `/api/districts`, which saves the districts (or asks which, for a split ZIP) and comes back to the ballot page. Before any lookup, a page that uses the saved place says "Showing: your saved place ([County])".
- `officialFor()` links a candidate to an official in the same state only when exactly one has that first and last name; `measureFor()` links a measure to ThePillory's page by its number (California's "Proposition N"; Washington's measure number).
- Errors (`CivicError`: `address`, `not-found`, `unavailable`, `no-key`) carry the HTTP status, never the request. An answer with no contests counts as no data: the page says so and links the state's official sample ballot lookup, with any polling places the answer did have.
- **The address is never stored or logged**, and the answer is sent `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. POST keeps it out of URLs and logs. Each connection may make `LOOKUPS_PER_DAY` (20) lookups a day: `ballot_lookups` (migration 0021) keeps only the daily-rotating visitor hash and the time. Before the sync applies the migration, there's no limit.
- Every page carries "Always confirm your ballot with your county election office." and the neutrality line.

## Ballot order

California orders candidates by a randomized alphabet drawn for each election (Elections Code 13112): last name first, letter by letter, then first and middle names. `ballotOrder()` applies it:
- **Statewide offices** rotate by Assembly district (13111): as drawn in AD 1, and in each later district the first name moves to the bottom. Your ballot uses the visitor's AD.
- **U.S. House** rotate among the ADs within the district, starting with its lowest-numbered AD; ThePillory doesn't have the AD list per congressional district, so it shows the drawn order and says the sample ballot may differ.
- **Legislature**: the drawn order, but a county can draw its own order where a district crosses county lines; the page says so.
- **Local contests** keep the county's list order. The tests check that it equals the computed order for every county contest (it does).
- A multi-word surname is read as the last word unless it starts with a particle (de, van, le, …), because the certified list doesn't mark it.

## Neutrality

Every candidate in a contest gets the same card with the same fields in ballot order: name, ballot designation, party preference as listed (partisan offices), the FEC filing (U.S. House), "Holds office now" with Platform, Votes and Funding links when the name matches exactly one official in D1, and the statement word for word or the same "no statement" line. Measure pages lead with the neutral official content: the official title and summary, what a Yes and a No vote mean, and the Legislative Analyst's fiscal estimate (with a link to the full analysis). The campaign arguments come after, collapsed, under the note "Written by each campaign, printed word for word from the official voter guide. Not written or checked by ThePillory or any government agency." They're in the guide's order in two matching panels: the supporters' argument with the opponents' rebuttal to it, then the opponents' argument with the supporters' rebuttal. Each part is labeled ("Supporters' argument", "Opponents' rebuttal", "Opponents' argument", "Supporters' rebuttal"), shown in full with who signed it, and a part the guide doesn't print says so.

On the official arguments page each column is an argument followed by the rebuttal to it, so a rebuttal is written by the other side. `arguments()` in `tools/build_elections.py` splits each column at every heading (an earlier version read a column as one argument, which merged each rebuttal, and its signers, into the argument above it). The pages write commas and hyphens as numeric entities (`&#44;`, `grid&#45;50`) and put some bulleted lists outside paragraphs; the parser decodes the first and keeps each list item as its own paragraph, marked "•". No endorsements, polls, predictions or race calls; results show the feed's numbers in ballot order with its "reporting" line and time.

## Results

`sosResults()` reads the Secretary of State's feed only once `pollsClosed()` (8 p.m. Pacific on Election Day), kept at the edge for two minutes; before then the feed carries test numbers. County contests and judicial retention link to the county's and the Secretary of State's results pages.
